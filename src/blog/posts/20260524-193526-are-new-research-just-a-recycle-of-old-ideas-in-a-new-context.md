# Are new research just a recycle of old ideas in a new context?

<!-- more -->

A question I keep coming back to: when something feels *new* in research, how
much of it is genuinely new, and how much is an old idea wearing the clothes of
a new context?

Attention wasn't invented in 2017 — alignment models in machine translation
were already doing soft lookups years before. Backprop is 1980s. Even
"in-context learning" rhymes with ideas about analogy and case-based reasoning
that are decades old. What changed was the *context*: more compute, more data,
a different substrate to run the old idea on.

Maybe that's the point. An idea is cheap; the context that makes it *work* is
expensive. The recycle isn't lazy — moving an idea into a context where it
finally pays off is itself the contribution. The transformer paper's value
wasn't "attention exists," it was "attention, alone, at this scale, is enough."

So the honest answer is probably: yes, mostly recycled — but recycling into the
right context is most of what progress actually is. The rare truly-new idea is
the exception, and even it stands on a stack of recontextualized old ones.

Worth keeping in mind next time I feel like I haven't had an "original" idea:
the value might be in spotting *which old idea belongs in this new context*.
